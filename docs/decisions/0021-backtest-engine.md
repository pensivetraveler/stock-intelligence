# ADR 0021 — Backtest engine (Phase 5B, stage B1)

- **Status:** accepted; B1 (engine) and B2 (screen) implemented
- **Date:** 2026-10-21

## Context

SYSTEM_SPEC §11 asks for deterministic, reproducible backtests:

- strategies written in Python and as declarative visual rules;
- signals computed on finalized bars and filled at the next bar's open, with fees and slippage;
- Decimal accounting;
- every input pinned, with immutable stored runs;
- no future-data access;
- fills kept apart from real ledgers.

On 2026-10-21 the user chose:

- Python strategies in the first release, alongside the rule builder;
- multi-symbol portfolios;
- two PRs: B1 for the engine and B2 for the screen.

## Decision

### Strategies (versioned, immutable)

- **Registry:** a strategy has an ID and numbered versions. A version is never edited; a change creates the next version. Each version stores its definition and the SHA-256 of that definition.
- **Rules strategy (`rules`):** a declarative JSON definition that the engine evaluates. It never runs code.
  - Entry and exit conditions combine `all`/`any` with `cross_above`, `cross_below`, `gt`, `gte`, `lt` and `lte`.
  - Operands are indicator outputs, bar prices or constants.
  - Optional stop-loss and take-profit percentages.
  - Sizing is a percentage of equity, a fixed cash amount or a fixed number of shares, with a maximum number of open positions.
- **Python strategy (`python`):** the user's code defines `on_bar(ctx)` and returns order intents.
  - **What `ctx` offers:** the instrument ID, the bar time, the current bar, this instrument's past final bars (`history`, `closes`), `indicator(id, output, **params)` over those bars, the strategy's `params`, the position, cash, equity and the number of open positions.
  - **Intents:** `ctx.buy(quantity=…)`, `ctx.buy(cash=…)` or `ctx.buy(percent_equity=…)`, and `ctx.sell()` (everything) or `ctx.sell(quantity=…)`. An intent can only trade the instrument whose bar is being shown.
  - **Output:** anything the code prints is kept (up to 200 entries) with the results as `logs`.
  - **Isolated process:** the code runs in a separate process with an empty environment (no keys or tokens), its own temporary working directory, CPU-time, file-size and open-file limits, and a wall-clock timeout.
  - **Audit hook:** a Python audit hook installed before the user's code refuses network sockets, subprocesses, `os.system`, writes outside the working directory and imports of `ctypes`.
  - **One bar at a time:** the engine sends bars one by one, so the process never holds a future bar to peek at.
  - **Limitation:** this is a guard against accidents, not a security boundary against deliberately hostile code on the same machine.
  - **Approval:** code generated by an Agent must still be approved before it runs (PROJECT_INSTRUCTIONS); that flow arrives with Phase 5.

### Indicators

- **Python indicators** (`services/backtest/indicators.py`): SMA, EMA, WMA, RSI, MACD, Bollinger, ATR, stochastic, VWAP and OBV. They are causal: the value at bar _i_ depends only on bars up to _i_, and a test checks that appending future bars never changes earlier values.
- **Parity with the chart:** a shared fixture (`tests/fixtures/indicators/parity.json`) holds bars and expected outputs. The Python and TypeScript implementations must both reproduce it, so the backtest and the chart agree.
- **Warm-up:** values during warm-up are `null`. A condition that reads a `null` is false.

### Engine (`services/backtest/engine.py`)

- **Portfolio:** several instruments, one currency (USD or KRW, which must match every instrument), long only, no margin, whole shares.
- **Event order** at each bar time, in time order across instruments:
  1. Fill orders queued at the previous close, at this bar's open, adjusted for slippage.
  2. Check stops and targets against this bar's high and low.
  3. Mark positions to the close.
  4. If the bar is final, evaluate signals at its close and queue orders for the next bar of that instrument.
- **No same-bar fills:** an order is never filled at the bar that signalled it.
- **Final bars only:** a bar that is not final never produces a signal.
- **Stop and target in the same bar:** the order of high and low inside a bar is unknown, so the stop is assumed (the conservative case) and the fill is flagged `ambiguous_stop_target`.
- **Costs:**
  - fee = notional × `fee_rate`, plus `fee_per_order`;
  - slippage moves the fill price against the order by `slippage_bps`.
- **Sizing:** fixed shares are fixed at the signal. A cash amount or a percentage of equity (equity at the signalling close) becomes shares at the fill: the budget ÷ (fill price × (1 + `fee_rate`)), after `fee_per_order`, rounded down. A budget larger than the cash available is reduced to the cash and the fill is noted `reduced_to_available_cash`.
- **One position per instrument:** a buy while holding (or with a buy already queued) is rejected; a sell may close part or all of a position.
- **Rejections** are recorded with their reason: `insufficient_cash` (fixed shares that cost more than the cash, or no cash left), `quantity_rounds_to_zero`, `position_limit`, `already_in_position`, `no_position`, `duplicate`, `other_instrument`, `invalid_size`, `invalid_quantity` and `invalid_intent`.
- **Orders still queued at the end** are reported as `unfilled_at_end`.
- **End of run:** open positions are not sold; they are valued at the last close and reported as unrealized.
- **Benchmark:** equal-weight buy-and-hold of the universe over the same bars, with no costs.
- **Metrics** (each definition is recorded with the result):
  - net and gross return;
  - maximum drawdown;
  - trade count, win rate, average win and loss, profit factor;
  - exposure: the share of bar times with any open position;
  - turnover: traded notional ÷ average equity;
  - a per-symbol breakdown.

### Data and reproducibility

- **Data source:** bars come through the same bar service as the charts, with the market router. Intraday runs are read session by session (at most 60 sessions per instrument); daily and weekly runs read the whole range.
- **Pinned inputs:** a run stores each instrument's source, feed, adjustment and dataset revision, coverage, missing intervals and the count of non-final bars, plus a SHA-256 of every input bar.
- **Warnings:** gaps, non-final bars, raw (unadjusted) prices and the survivorship limit (the universe is the user's list as of today) are reported as warnings, never hidden.
- **Suspected splits:** with raw prices, a jump from one close to the next open of about a whole ratio (×2 or more, within 6%) is named by date in the warnings and in the pinned inputs (`suspected_split_dates`). The bars are never changed. In a live check on 2026-10-21, NVDA's 10:1 split on 2024-06-10 turned a held position into a −86.6% stop-loss on unadjusted Alpaca prices; it is now named in the warnings.
- **Determinism:** the engine has no randomness; the run's seed is recorded for future use. Identical inputs give byte-identical results, and a test checks this.

### Jobs, storage and API

- **Storage:** `SIW_DATA_DIR/backtests.sqlite3` holds strategies, versions and runs. A run's configuration is a snapshot fixed at submission. Status moves `queued → running → succeeded | failed | cancelled`, with progress and a failure log. Results are stored as one immutable JSON document per finished run.
- **Execution:** one background worker thread runs jobs one at a time. Cancellation is checked at every step, and a cancelled Python strategy's process is killed.
- **API:**
  - `POST /v1/backtests/strategies` (a new strategy, or with `strategy_id` its next version; an unchanged definition keeps its version), `GET /v1/backtests/strategies` and `GET /v1/backtests/strategies/{id}?version=`;
  - `POST /v1/backtests/runs` (answers 202 with the queued run), `GET /v1/backtests/runs`, `GET /v1/backtests/runs/{id}`, `GET /v1/backtests/runs/{id}/results` and `POST /v1/backtests/runs/{id}/cancel`.
- **Run configuration:** 1 to 20 instruments, all in the run's currency (KRW for Korean listings, USD otherwise); a timeframe (`Nm`, `1d` or `1w`); start and end dates in exchange time; initial capital; `fee_rate` (at most 0.1), `fee_per_order`, `slippage_bps` (at most 1,000); an optional `as_of_utc` cutoff; and a seed.
- **Without `SIW_DATA_DIR`** the endpoints answer 503 and nothing is stored. A run left `running` when the app stopped is marked failed at the next start.
- **Contracts:** new `backtest_strategy`, `backtest_strategy_list_response`, `backtest_run`, `backtest_run_list_response` and `backtest_results`. The existing `backtest_fill` and `backtest_run_config` now also allow KRW, and a fill may carry its `fee` and `notes`.
- **Separation:** backtest fills live only in `backtests.sqlite3`. They never touch the journal or any ledger.

## Screen (B2)

- **Where:** a "Backtest" button on the right rail opens a large window over the charts. Esc or ✕ closes it.
- **Strategy editor:**
  - Rules are edited in a form: entry and exit conditions combined with all/any; operands are the 10 engine indicators (with their parameters and outputs), a bar price or a value; plus stop/target, sizing and max positions.
  - A saved rule nested deeper than the form can show is displayed as JSON and run exactly as saved.
  - Python strategies are edited as code with JSON parameters, under a note that the code runs isolated and should be reviewed first.
  - The API, not the screen, decides whether a definition is valid; its message is shown.
- **Run:**
  - Instruments are chosen from the watchlist (configured and added). Only the instruments shown can be selected, and US and Korean stocks cannot be mixed in one run.
  - "Save and run" saves the editor first; an unchanged definition keeps its version.
  - Runs are polled every 1.5 s while one is queued or running, and can be cancelled.
- **Results:**
  - warnings first, then the metric cards (each card's tooltip is the engine's definition);
  - the equity curve against buy-and-hold, with the drawdown below on a linked time axis;
  - the per-instrument table, trades, and orders not filled (with their reasons);
  - pinned inputs and the strategy's output, behind disclosure toggles.
- **On the charts:** "Show fills on charts" draws that run's fills on every chart showing the instrument, as pink `BT` markers. A chip on the chart says they are simulated and hides them. They are never journal entries.
- **Compared with real fills:** for each instrument, backtest buys and sells (count and average price) are shown next to the user's real executions (manual, imported and broker API) within about a day of the run's dates. This is a reference, not a trade-by-trade match; real executions without a known time are left out.

## Alternatives considered

- **Run the engine in the browser and reuse the TypeScript indicators:** this avoids porting indicators, but gives no isolated Python execution, no queued and cancellable server jobs and no Decimal accounting.
- **Use a container or `sandbox-exec` for Python:** this isolates the code better, but adds a platform-specific dependency for a single-user local tool. It can be revisited if strategies from other people are ever run.
- **Infer the order of high and low inside a bar:** forbidden by the spec. The conservative choice is used instead and flagged.

## Revisit when

- Short selling, margin, multiple currencies (FX) or limit and stop entry orders are needed.
- Walk-forward or out-of-sample workflows are added (spec §11.4, later stage).
- Strategies written by others, or by an Agent without review, are to be run: a stronger sandbox would then be needed.
